PROBLEM STATEMENT & PROPOSED SOLUTION

Citizens need a clearer view of complaint progress

The issue is not only how to file a complaint; it is how to follow the work through to a reliable outcome.

Resolved
Submitted
Assigned
In progress
Evidence added

Follow-up

Current experience can be fragmented

  • Status updates may be brief
  • Repair evidence may be hard to compare with the original report
  • Repeat issues may be reported as new tickets
  • Citizens may not know what happens next

What JanPramaan adds

  • One progress timeline from submission to follow-up
  • Updates from the responsible department
  • Original and completion evidence in one record
  • Citizen feedback or dispute after a repair claim

The intended outcome

Citizens can see who is handling the issue, what stage it is at, what evidence was submitted and whether a repeat report has appeared.

Key principle: show progress transparently, but label evidence as “submitted”, “under review” or “verified”—not all as automatically verified.

EXISTING SOLUTIONS & DIFFERENTIATION

Existing platforms already track complaints—so what is our gap?

We should not claim that tracking is new. JanPramaan proposes a focused layer for progress visibility, evidence history and repeat-issue follow-up.

Differentiation to validate: one issue record + full progress timeline + repair evidence + repeat-issue follow-up in one simple interface.

Sources: CPGRAMS, Swachh City / Swachhata, Election Commission of India cVIGIL (links in section 8).

INNOVATION / USP

One complaint.One live timeline.One evidence trail.

JanPramaan does not stop at “Resolved”: it shows the complaint journey, records the evidence behind the repair, and links the same problem if it returns.

1

Live Progress Timeline

Submitted → assigned → work in progress → evidence added → citizen review → follow-up. Each update has a time and responsible role.

2

Evidence-linked Closure

Keep the original report beside the repair photo and metadata. Citizens can flag “not fixed” or “evidence unclear”.

3

Repeat-Issue Memory

Link likely repeat reports to the same location and issue history; flag recurring failures for human review.

Citizens know what stage their complaint is at.

Departments can see evidence and unresolved follow-up.

Ward-level reports can reveal recurring problem locations.

Honest claim: the combination is our proposed differentiation; it must be tested against existing municipal workflows before claiming exclusivity.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Simple web architecture for a working prototype

Proposed architecture; components can be implemented with common web-development tools.

React

Builds the citizen portal and department dashboard.

FastAPI

Workflow and APIs: auth, roles, status updates, notifications.

Supabase

Issues and timeline (PostgreSQL), plus file storage for photos.

Optional AI

Similarity flags for likely duplicates. Human review decides.

MVP choice: React + FastAPI + Supabase. AI matching is optional; human review controls disputed evidence and closure.

FEASIBILITY, IMPACT & SCALABILITY

Start with one ward and measure whether the workflow helps

No custom hardware or government integration is required to demonstrate the concept.

MVP scope

  • Complaint form + tracking ID
  • Progress timeline
  • Department update screen
  • Evidence upload + review
  • Repeat-issue history

Risks & safeguards

  • Fake/old photos → metadata checks
  • False match → human confirmation
  • Privacy → access controls and redaction
  • No API → simulated department dashboard

Practical rollout

  1. Build a sample prototype
  2. Test one category (potholes / garbage)
  3. Pilot with a campus or ward partner
  4. Integrate only with permission

Proposed pilot metrics

Progress visibility

% complaints with stage updates

Evidence quality

% closure claims with usable proof

Repeat issue rate

Confirmed repeat reports within 30 days

Review accuracy

% flags confirmed by human reviewers

Potential impact: citizens know the current stage; departments get a clearer evidence trail; repeat locations become easier to spot; scale from ward → municipality → partner networks.

All benefits are expected outcomes, not measured results; a pilot is needed to establish effectiveness.
